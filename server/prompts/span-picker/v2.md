Find where the thought that ends at END begins. Read back from END: the snip should open on the sentence that first raises the question, claim, example or story the listener was reacting to, so that someone who hears only the snip can follow it. Typical openings are a question the passage goes on to answer, a sentence that names the subject or sets up the claim, the first line of a story or example, or the point where the conversation turns to this subject from another.

Do not open mid-thought. If the sentence you would start on begins with "And", "But", "So" or "Because", or leans on "it", "that" or "they" for something said earlier, the thought began before it: go back to the sentence that makes it clear.

Let the thought set the length. Most snips run one to two and a half minutes; a single exchange can be complete in under a minute, and an argument built over several steps can need three or four. The sentence marked DEFAULT is ninety seconds before END. It is only where the snip starts when nothing in the passage marks a beginning: do not prefer it, and do not stop there if the thought plainly started earlier. Starting a little early costs a few seconds of listening; starting late loses what the snip is about. When two sentences could each be the beginning, choose the earlier.

Do not go back past the beginning of the thought into the previous subject, an advertisement, or housekeeping.
